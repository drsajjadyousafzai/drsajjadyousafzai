medicalTerm: "Deep vein thrombosis (a blood clot in the leg)"
order: 3
iconKey: "clot"
summary: "A leg that becomes swollen, warm and painful on one side. Assessed and treated urgently."
insideBody: "Deep vein thrombosis means a blood clot has formed in one of the deep veins, usually in the calf or thigh. The clot blocks the blood trying to return to the heart, so the leg swells and hurts. The main danger is that a piece of the clot can break off and travel to the lungs, which is serious."
signs:
  - "Swelling in one leg that comes on over hours or a day or two"
  - "Pain or cramp in the calf, often when standing or walking"
  - "The skin feeling warm, and looking red or slightly blue"
  - "The leg feeling tight and tender to touch"
treatment: "First the clot is confirmed, usually with an ultrasound scan. Treatment is blood-thinning medicine that stops the clot growing and lets the body clear it safely. In selected cases other treatments are used to remove or break down a large clot. You will also be checked to understand why the clot formed."
recovery:
  - "Early days: blood-thinning medicine is started, and gentle walking is usually encouraged."
  - "Weeks: swelling settles slowly. A support stocking is often advised."
  - "Months: blood thinners are usually continued for a set period, decided for your case."
  - "Follow-up checks make sure the leg is recovering and the medicine dose is right."

    answer: "It should be seen quickly, the same day if possible. A clot can grow or travel to the lungs. If your leg is suddenly swollen and painful, do not wait. Seek care straight away."
  - question: "How long will I need to take blood thinners?"
    answer: "This depends on why the clot formed. Some people need them for a few months, others for longer. Dr. Sajjad will explain the plan and the reasons for it."
  - question: "Can I fly or travel with a blood clot?"
    answer: "Long journeys can raise the risk while a clot is active. Ask Dr. Sajjad before making travel plans so he can advise on what is safe for you."

A sudden, painful, swollen leg should never be ignored. Seen early, a deep vein clot can be treated safely and the leg protected from long-term harm.
