Without blood flow, the tissue starts to be damaged within hours. This is one of the true emergencies in vascular surgery. The limb, and sometimes life, can depend on how quickly it is treated."
signs:
  - "A leg or arm that suddenly turns cold and pale"
  - "Sudden, severe pain in the limb"
  - "Numbness or weakness that comes on quickly"
  - "A pulse that can no longer be felt in the foot or wrist"
treatment: "This is treated as an emergency from the moment you arrive. Dr. Sajjad will assess the limb straight away and act quickly, which may mean an urgent operation to clear the blockage, medicine to break down the clot, or a combination of both, depending on what is found. Every hour matters, so treatment does not wait for a routine appointment slot."
recovery:
  - "Immediately after treatment: close monitoring in hospital to confirm the blood supply has returned."
  - "First days: the cause of the sudden blockage is investigated, for example the heart or the artery itself."
  - "Weeks: physiotherapy and wound care if any tissue was affected before treatment."
  - "Ongoing: medicine to lower the chance of another clot, and treatment of the underlying cause."
faqs:
  - question: "How is this different from ordinary peripheral artery disease?"
    answer: "Peripheral artery disease narrows the artery slowly over years. Acute limb ischaemia is a sudden, complete blockage, usually by a clot, and it is a same hour emergency, not something that can wait for a booked appointment."
  - question: "What happens if treatment is delayed?"
    answer: "Without blood flow, muscle and nerve tissue can be damaged within hours, and the limb can be lost. This is why a sudden cold, pale, painful leg or arm needs hospital care immediately, not the next day."
