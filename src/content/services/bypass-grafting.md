insideBody: "Some artery blockages are too long or too hard for a balloon and stent to open safely. In these cases, a bypass can be made: a graft, either a vein taken from elsewhere in your body or a synthetic tube, is joined above and below the blockage so blood flows around it, not through it. This restores blood flow to the leg or foot and can prevent amputation."

  - "A cold, pale or discoloured foot with weak or absent pulses"
treatment: "Dr. Sajjad first maps the blockage with scans to see if a bypass is the right option, or whether a balloon and stent can be used instead. If a bypass is needed, it is done as an operation, usually under anaesthesia, joining a graft above and below the blocked section. This is bigger surgery than a balloon procedure, and he will explain honestly what it involves before you agree to it."
recovery:
  - "First few days: a hospital stay while the leg and wound are monitored closely."
  - "First weeks: swelling in the leg is normal and settles gradually. Gentle walking is encouraged as advised."
  - "One to two months: the graft is checked with follow-up scans to confirm it is working well."
  - "Managing diabetes, blood pressure and stopping smoking all protect the graft afterwards."

When a blocked artery is too long to open from the inside, a bypass graft routes blood around it. This can save a leg that would otherwise be at risk of amputation.
